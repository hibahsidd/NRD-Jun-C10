30- and 90-Day Readmissions Among Heart Failure Patients With Stimulant (Predominantly Methamphetamine) Use Disorder

Analysis for RCOP NRD C10 - Nationwide Readmissions Database, 2016–2022

Author
Affiliation

Hibah Siddiqui

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble

  • Reference Studies:

    • Thyagaturu et al., 2023 (Curr Probl Cardiol; NRD 2016–2018)

  • HF definition sources:

    • AHRQ Prevention Quality Indicator 08, Heart Failure Admission Rate
    • Canonico et al., 2025 (Am J Manag Care; shift of HF principal diagnoses to I11.0/I13.x)

Objective: To compare unplanned all-cause readmission within 30 days of discharge between heart failure (HF) patients with stimulant (predominantly methamphetamine) use disorder and HF patients with no stimulant, cocaine or cannabis use disorder. Secondary objectives were to compare 90-day all-cause readmission and 30- and 90-day HF-specific readmission; to describe the ten most common causes of readmission in each group; to compare length of stay, inflation-adjusted cost and in-hospital mortality at the index admission and at readmission; to identify predictors of readmission within the stimulant group; and to test whether the association differed by sex.

Study design and data source: Retrospective cohort study using the 2016–2022 Nationwide Readmissions Database (NRD), sponsored by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality’s Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP). The NRD is a nationally representative, all-payer database of U.S. hospital discharges that links a patient’s admissions across hospitals within the same state and calendar year through a de-identified patient linkage number (NRD_VisitLink) and a timing variable (NRD_DaysToEvent). All years are coded in ICD-10-CM. Because linkage does not cross calendar years, every patient needs a full follow-up window inside the year of the index discharge, so two cohorts were built: a 30-day cohort (index discharges January–November) and a 90-day cohort (index discharges January–September).

Study population: Adults (age ≥ 18 years) with a non-elective hospitalization and a principal diagnosis of HF who were discharged alive. HF was defined as a principal ICD-10-CM diagnosis of I50.x (heart failure), I11.0 (hypertensive heart disease with HF), I13.0 or I13.2 (hypertensive heart and chronic kidney disease with HF), following the AHRQ Prevention Quality Indicator 08 definition of an HF hospitalization. Under ICD-10-CM coding guidelines, hypertension and heart failure are presumed to be linked, so patients with both conditions are coded with I11.0 or I13.x as the principal diagnosis and I50.x as a secondary code; in this cohort, approximately 80% of HF index admissions carried I11.0 or I13.x as the principal diagnosis, so an I50-only definition would have excluded most HF hospitalizations. Rheumatic heart failure (I09.81, also in the AHRQ definition) was not included. Every eligible HF admission was treated as an index admission, so a patient could contribute more than one index admission. Exclusions were applied in this order: age < 18 years; elective admission; December discharge (30-day cohort) or October–December discharge (90-day cohort); in-hospital death at the index admission; missing NRD_VisitLink, length of stay, age, sex or discharge disposition; any cocaine (F14.x) or cannabis (F12.x) use disorder code; and unspecified stimulant use (F15.9x) without an F15.1x or F15.2x code. Unweighted and weighted counts after each step are reported in the cohort flow tables below (Figure 1).

Exposure: Stimulant use disorder, defined as at least one ICD-10-CM F15.1x (other stimulant abuse) or F15.2x (other stimulant dependence) code in any of the diagnosis positions of the index admission, with no cocaine (F14.x) or cannabis (F12.x) use disorder code, so that the exposed group is stimulant-only. The comparator group had no F15, F14 or F12 code of any kind. Codes were matched on their first four characters so that sub-codes added between fiscal years were captured automatically. F15 codes do not separate methamphetamine from other amphetamine-type stimulants (or, rarely, caffeine), so the exposure is described as stimulant (predominantly methamphetamine) use disorder. The NRD has no present-on-admission indicator, so exposure and covariates mean “coded at the index admission”.

Primary outcome: Unplanned all-cause readmission within 30 days of index discharge, defined as the first non-elective admission for any principal diagnosis within the window. Readmissions were identified with the NRD linkage variables; only the first readmission in the window was counted.

Secondary outcomes: 90-day all-cause readmission (90-day cohort, same definition); 30- and 90-day HF-specific readmission (a first readmission with a principal diagnosis of I50.x, I11.0, I13.0 or I13.2); the ten most common principal diagnoses at readmission (ranked overall, with the within-group weighted percentage for each group); length of stay and inflation-adjusted cost at the index admission and at readmission; in-hospital mortality at readmission; and days from index discharge to readmission. Index in-hospital deaths are reported as a count in the cohort flow only, because they are excluded from the cohort.

Covariates: Age (continuous, modelled per 10 years; age group shown descriptively), sex, admission day (weekday vs. weekend), primary expected payer, median household income quartile by ZIP code, hospital bed size, hospital location/teaching status, index discharge disposition (routine home/self-care, home health care, skilled nursing or other facility, short-term hospital, against medical advice; the rare “alive, destination unknown” category, <0.1% of admissions, was set to missing), HF type, discharge year (categorical), and 19 comorbidities identified from any diagnosis position at the index admission: hypertension (I10–I13, I15, I16, I1A), diabetes (E08–E11, E13), obesity (E66 excluding E66.3; Z68.3x–Z68.4x), coronary artery disease (I20, I25, Z95.1, Z95.5, Z98.61), obstructive sleep apnea (G47.33), atrial fibrillation/flutter (I48), COPD (J41–J44), prior stroke/TIA (Z86.73, I69), chronic kidney disease stage 3–5 without ESRD (N18.3–N18.5), end-stage renal disease (N18.6, Z99.2), peripheral vascular disease (I70, I73.1, I73.8, I73.9), anemia (D50–D64), depression (F32 excluding F32.81, F33), anxiety disorder (F41), stress-related disorder (F43), nicotine dependence (F17.2), alcohol use disorder (F10.1, F10.2), opioid use disorder (F11.1, F11.2) and chronic hepatitis B or C (B18.0–B18.2, B19.20, B19.21). HF type was classified from I50 codes in any diagnosis position (for I11.0/I13.x index admissions, from the secondary I50 code) as combined (I50.4x, or both I50.2x and I50.3x), systolic (I50.2x), diastolic (I50.3x) or other/unspecified. Because I11.0 and I13.x are themselves hypertension codes, all patients with a hypertensive HF principal diagnosis are flagged as having hypertension. The Charlson Comorbidity Index is reported in Table 1 only and was not included in the models, because its components overlap with the individual comorbidity flags.

Cost: Hospital costs were estimated by applying HCUP hospital-specific cost-to-charge ratios to reported total charges, then adjusting to 2022 dollars using the Consumer Price Index for medical care. All reported dollar figures represent estimated hospital costs, not billed charges.

Statistical analysis: All estimates accounted for the NRD’s complex survey design (discharge weights, hospital-level clustering and stratification, with year-qualified strata and clusters for the pooled 2016–2022 data), so that results are nationally representative. Baseline characteristics were compared between groups as weighted medians (interquartile range) for continuous variables and weighted percentages for categorical variables, using the design-based Wilcoxon rank-sum test and the Rao-Scott chi-square test. Readmission was compared using survey-weighted logistic regression (quasibinomial): an unadjusted model and a model adjusted for all covariates, reported as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals for stimulant use disorder vs. the comparator. The same models were fitted for 90-day all-cause and 30- and 90-day HF-specific readmission. Predictors of 30-day readmission within the stimulant group were assessed with the same covariates. Effect modification by sex was tested with an exposure-by-sex interaction term in the adjusted 30-day model, and sex-stratified adjusted odds ratios are reported. The distribution of readmission diagnoses was compared between groups with a Rao-Scott test (top 10 diagnoses plus all others). Length of stay, cost and days to readmission were compared as weighted medians with a design-based Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Analyses were complete-case; median household income missingness is shown as its own category. Tests were two-sided with alpha = 0.05. Consistent with HCUP data-use guidance, estimates based on fewer than 11 unweighted discharges are not reported.

Sensitivity analyses (30-day all-cause readmission): (1) strict exposure definition (F15.10, F15.11, F15.20, F15.21 only); (2) restricted index definition limited to a principal diagnosis of I50.x (the definition in the original analysis plan); (3) reference-study exposure definition (any F15.1x or F15.2x code regardless of co-occurring cocaine, cannabis or opioid use disorder), with the same comparator; (4) excluding discharges against medical advice; and (5) restricting to 2019–2022, the years after the reference study.

Note for Tables 5b and 5d: P-values are from a design-based Wilcoxon rank-sum test, which compares full distributions rather than medians; given the large sample size, a significant p-value can occur even when medians and IQRs are nearly identical and should be interpreted alongside the reported effect size rather than as a standalone indicator of clinical relevance.

Note on effect size: The reference study reported hazard ratios from Cox models; this analysis reports odds ratios, so the two should be compared for direction and magnitude only and not treated as equivalent.
